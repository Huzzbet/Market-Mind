# Market Mind — Flagship Research Edition
**Saturday 14 November 2026 | Australian and US markets | Research horizon: 6–18 months**

> **Editorial status:** First edition. This is independent market research and a watchlist, not personal financial advice. Prices and valuation multiples are deliberately omitted where a reliable, timestamped market quote was not verified for this edition. No target prices, broker consensus revisions, institutional flows or probability estimates are invented.

## Executive view

The market is confronting a difficult combination: inflation has proved sticky, long-term yields are elevated, and the AI investment cycle has suffered a leadership shock at its most influential company. The result is a higher hurdle rate for long-duration assets and a greater premium on cash generation, balance-sheet resilience and demonstrable returns on capital.

Our central judgement is **selective risk-off, not a wholesale rejection of growth**. The strongest opportunities may be businesses whose earnings can compound despite tighter financial conditions, while investors demand more proof that enormous AI infrastructure spending will convert into durable free cash flow.

### Three things that matter most

1. **Rates are the valuation gatekeeper.** The RBA lifted the cash rate to 4.85% in November. Its November Statement expects headline inflation to peak at 4.5% in the December 2026 quarter, remain above 3% until late 2027 and return to the midpoint of the target range around mid-2028. This is a material headwind for highly leveraged companies and long-duration equity valuations.
2. **The AI thesis is intact, but the leadership premium is being repriced.** Jensen Huang resigned as NVIDIA CEO on 12 November, with CFO Colette Kress appointed interim CEO. The SEC filing confirms the transition. This is a company-specific governance and strategic uncertainty, not evidence that AI demand has disappeared.
3. **Cash conversion is becoming the differentiator.** Alphabet's Cloud growth is exceptional, but its Q3 capex was US$51.4bn and trailing-12-month free cash flow was US$19.7bn. Amazon's AWS growth is strong, but Reuters reported a trailing-12-month free-cash-flow outflow of US$42.4bn amid a capex surge. Investors need to judge the return on each incremental dollar of AI infrastructure spend.

### What the market may be missing

The distinction between *AI demand* and *AI equity returns* is critical. Demand for compute can continue growing while returns to infrastructure investors disappoint if capital expenditure, depreciation, power costs and competition rise faster than monetisation. We should track revenue growth, gross margin, operating margin, free cash flow and incremental returns on invested capital—not just AI headlines or capex announcements.

## The Rotation — what capital is favouring

**Observed on Friday in Australia:** utilities outperformed; materials and property underperformed. This is consistent with a defensive tilt during a global yield and technology shock. It is one session, not proof of a durable rotation.

**Observed in the US:** technology and semiconductors led the decline, with the semiconductor index down 4%. Reuters reported energy shares had been relatively resilient earlier in the week, while the dollar and short-dated Treasury yields strengthened.

**Our interpretation:** higher yields pressure long-duration growth and property valuations. Commodity-linked equities face a two-sided outlook: supply constraints and electrification/AI demand can support copper, but a stronger US dollar, higher real yields and weaker risk appetite can overwhelm the structural story in the short term.

## The Opportunity — six companies to research

These are ranked research candidates, not buy recommendations. The ranking reflects the quality and clarity of the current business thesis, not a verified expected return. A valuation entry point has not been established in this edition.

### Australia

#### 1. BHP Group (ASX: BHP) — copper exposure with scale
**Thesis:** BHP offers large-scale exposure to copper, a commodity with long-term demand drivers in electrification, power infrastructure and data centres, while retaining a diversified iron ore and other commodities base.

**Evidence:** FY26 underlying EBITDA was approximately US$33bn, net debt fell below US$9bn, and copper contributed more than half of underlying EBITDA for the first time. Its September-quarter FY27 operational review reported copper production down 19% year on year to 401kt, while realised copper prices were up 53% to US$7.01/lb. FY27 copper production guidance remained 1.65–1.80Mt.

**Catalysts:** copper price and treatment-charge dynamics; operational recovery after grade and maintenance impacts; progress on the Jansen potash project; development milestones across the copper portfolio.

**Risks:** China demand and iron ore pricing; execution and grade risk at major copper assets; commodity volatility; project capex; operational safety.

**Valuation lens:** normalise earnings using mid-cycle copper and iron ore assumptions rather than capitalising spot prices. Stress-test the equity against lower copper prices, higher costs and delayed project delivery. A high-quality asset base does not make the shares attractive at every price.

**What invalidates the thesis:** sustained copper price weakness, repeated production disappointments, material cost inflation or capital allocation that fails to earn returns above the cost of capital.

Sources: BHP FY26 results (18 Aug 2026), BHP September-quarter FY27 operational review (20 Oct 2026).

#### 2. Goodman Group (ASX: GMG) — data-centre infrastructure and industrial property
**Thesis:** Goodman is positioned at the intersection of logistics property, urban land scarcity and data-centre infrastructure. The power-constrained development pipeline may be a competitive advantage, but the valuation must reflect capital intensity and interest-rate sensitivity.

**Evidence:** FY26 operating profit rose 15.7% to A$2.675bn; operating earnings per security rose 10.1% to 129.9 cents. FY27 operating earnings growth guidance is 9%. Work in progress was A$19.7bn, with data centres representing 78% of development WIP. Portfolio occupancy was 95.6%, and like-for-like net property income growth was 4.0%.

**Catalysts:** conversion of secured power and development WIP into completed, income-producing assets; leasing and customer commitments; delivery against FY27 earnings guidance.

**Risks:** higher discount rates and bond yields; construction cost and delivery risk; power availability; customer concentration; development yields compressing; a premium valuation leaving little room for execution slippage.

**Valuation lens:** assess price against operating earnings per security, net tangible assets, development yields and implied cap rates. Apply a higher discount rate and lower terminal growth in downside scenarios. Do not use statutory profit alone because property revaluations can distort earnings.

**What invalidates the thesis:** material slippage in data-centre delivery, falling development yields, weaker occupancy or a persistent rise in financing costs that erodes returns.

Source: Goodman FY26 results (20 Aug 2026).

#### 3. CSL (ASX: CSL) — turnaround with a measurable earnings hurdle
**Thesis:** CSL is a quality plasma and biotechnology franchise attempting to reset operations after a difficult FY26. The opportunity is a recovery in margins and earnings; the risk is that the recovery takes longer or costs more than management expects.

**Evidence:** FY26 revenue was US$15.8bn, down 1%; underlying NPATA was US$3.1bn, down 2%; operating cash flow was US$3.5bn. Reported NPAT was a US$2.6bn loss after impairments and restructuring costs. Management's FY27 outlook is revenue broadly in line with FY26 and underlying NPAT growth of approximately 5%. The company reported A$176m of transformation savings achieved by FY26 year end, ahead of its target.

**Catalysts:** delivery of the FY27 earnings-growth target; plasma collection and manufacturing productivity; commercial performance of newer therapies; evidence that transformation savings translate into recurring margin improvement.

**Risks:** execution, plasma collection costs and donor economics; product competition; weaker immunisation rates affecting Seqirus; Vifor revenue pressure from generic competition and other product-specific headwinds.

**Valuation lens:** use normalised underlying earnings and free cash flow, excluding one-off restructuring and impairment noise but not ignoring recurring costs. Require evidence of sustainable growth before paying a premium multiple.

**What invalidates the thesis:** failure to deliver FY27 growth, further guidance reductions, deterioration in plasma economics or repeated execution issues.

Source: CSL FY26 results and FY27 outlook (18 Aug 2026).

### United States

#### 4. Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOGL / GOOG) — AI monetisation across Search and Cloud
**Thesis:** Alphabet is one of the clearest examples of an incumbent monetising AI across an existing distribution and infrastructure base. Its latest quarter showed that AI investment is supporting demand rather than simply cannibalising the core business.

**Evidence:** Q3 2026 revenue grew 25% to US$127.6bn; Google Cloud revenue grew 103% to US$30.7bn; operating income rose 30%, with a 31.8% operating margin. Search revenue grew 12%. However, Q3 capex was US$51.4bn and trailing-12-month free cash flow was US$19.7bn. Management maintained FY26 capex guidance of US$195–205bn and said capex would rise significantly in 2027.

**Catalysts:** continued Cloud growth; enterprise AI adoption; Search monetisation and query growth; evidence that infrastructure spending produces attractive incremental returns.

**Risks:** capex and depreciation burden; AI search changing advertising economics; competition; regulation and antitrust; capacity constraints and power costs.

**Valuation lens:** value Search, Cloud and other businesses separately where possible. Focus on owner earnings after infrastructure investment, not headline revenue growth alone. A sum-of-the-parts approach should explicitly stress-test Cloud margins and AI capex returns.

**What invalidates the thesis:** sustained deterioration in Search monetisation, Cloud growth deceleration with poor margins, or persistent negative free cash flow caused by infrastructure spending.

Source: Alphabet Q3 2026 results and earnings call (28 Oct 2026).

#### 5. Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) — enterprise distribution and recurring cloud economics
**Thesis:** Microsoft's broad enterprise distribution, cloud platform and productivity ecosystem give it multiple routes to monetise AI. It is a high-quality business to monitor during the AI volatility, but valuation and capital intensity remain central.

**Evidence:** FY26 revenue was US$331.8bn, up 18%; operating income rose 21% to US$155.2bn; diluted EPS was US$17.95, up 32% on a GAAP basis. The company also disclosed that several discrete items benefited Q4 EPS, including a US$3.2bn gain from an investment in Anthropic and lower-than-expected retirement-program costs. Those items should not be treated as recurring operating earnings.

**Catalysts:** Azure growth and capacity expansion; Copilot adoption and monetisation; AI workloads moving into production; evidence of incremental gross profit per AI customer.

**Risks:** capex, depreciation and power costs; cloud competition; AI monetisation below expectations; security and service reliability; regulatory exposure.

**Valuation lens:** separate recurring operating earnings from investment gains and other one-offs. Track capex relative to operating cash flow and free cash flow, plus Azure growth and margins. A premium multiple requires sustained high returns on incremental AI capital.

**What invalidates the thesis:** a sustained slowdown in cloud growth, weak paid AI adoption, declining margins or capital intensity that materially reduces free cash flow per share.

Source: Microsoft FY26 annual report and Q4 results (29 Jul 2026).

#### 6. Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) — AWS growth versus cash-flow intensity
**Thesis:** Amazon's AWS business is accelerating and retail margins are improving, but the scale of AI infrastructure investment makes free cash flow the critical variable. This is an attractive business to research, not yet a clean cash-flow story.

**Evidence:** Q3 2026 net sales rose 12% to US$201.9bn; operating income reached US$28.1bn, a record 13.9% operating margin; AWS sales rose 39% to US$46bn and AWS operating income was US$17.8bn. Amazon guided to 2026 cash capex of approximately US$220bn. Reuters reported trailing-12-month free cash flow had turned to a US$42.4bn outflow as capex accelerated.

**Catalysts:** AWS capacity coming online; conversion of AI demand into durable contracted revenue; retail logistics productivity; advertising growth normalising after Prime Day timing effects.

**Risks:** capex overshoot, depreciation, power and chip costs; AWS competition; low or negative free cash flow; consumer demand; execution risk from a very large infrastructure build-out.

**Valuation lens:** use a sum-of-the-parts approach and model AWS separately. Stress-test the return on incremental data-centre capital and the timing of cash conversion. Avoid relying solely on operating income while free cash flow is deeply negative.

**What invalidates the thesis:** AWS growth slowing materially, incremental returns on infrastructure capital falling, or free cash flow remaining structurally negative without a credible path to recovery.

Sources: Amazon Q3 2026 results (29 Oct 2026); Reuters analysis (29 Oct 2026).

## The Signal — NVIDIA and the AI risk premium

NVIDIA is not on this edition's Conviction List. Its CEO transition is confirmed: Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. The SEC filing records the board's acceptance of his resignation. Reuters reported the departure has raised questions about US chip-export policy and the broader AI supply chain. The US Department of Justice said it did not know whether the Chinese businesswoman at the centre of the reporting was a spy; that allegation should not be repeated as an established fact.

**Our judgement:** the issue is not simply a one-day share-price reaction. It is the potential loss of a founder-CEO who has been central to product strategy, customer relationships and the geopolitical positioning of the company. Until succession, board oversight and strategic continuity become clearer, NVIDIA deserves a higher governance risk premium. The broader AI thesis should be tested through cloud demand, hyperscaler capex, chip supply chains and customer returns—not assumed to be broken.
